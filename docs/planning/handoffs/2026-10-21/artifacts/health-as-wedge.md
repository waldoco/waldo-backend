
# Health as Waldo's wedge
2026-10-19 · @Shivansh Fulper
## Verdict
Health is a strong wedge for Waldo worldwide, on one condition: Waldo is the agent that acts on how you are, not another health coach. People care: longer life, less stress and better sleep are the top three health goals across 27 countries (51%, 44% and 40%). More than 230 million people ask ChatGPT health questions every week. But coaching and health chat are already crowded, and nobody turns body state into actions across calendar, food and care. Muse comes closest, and only in the US.
-
Market: about 21 million people worldwide wear a tracker, would share its data with an AI, and already pay for a health or AI subscription. Half are in the US. India has the largest need but the thinnest spending on health software.
-
Mental health: 40% of employees felt a lot of stress yesterday, and 1 billion+ people live with a mental disorder. Waldo's lane is sleep, workload and recovery. AI therapy is now banned in several US states.
-
Moat: horizontal agents can copy data access and health chat in weeks. What they cannot copy quickly is months of one owner's baselines, corrections and acted-on plans.
-
Plugin: later, as distribution. The plugin seats inside big agents went to companies that own scarce data.
-
Biggest risks: Google adding actions to its coach; only 24% of people trust tech companies with biometric data; and wearables' value over a one-tap self-report is unproven.
Next step: a 30-day, 20-owner pilot that tests the last risk directly (see What to test next).
## Do people care
Yes. Living longer, less stress and better sleep are the top three health goals worldwide, and people already spend on them. Health is also among the most common things people ask AI about. The catch is trust: only a quarter would hand biometric data to a tech company.

But horizontal assistants already serve health Q&A at that scale, so answering questions cannot be Waldo's edge.
## Mental health and burnout
The need is huge and mostly unmet. More than a billion people live with a mental disorder, and 40% of employees felt a lot of stress yesterday. People already turn to AI for help. But the law now bars AI from acting as a therapist, so Waldo's lane is sleep, workload and recovery, not treatment.
### How big the need is
| Measure | Figure | Source | | People with a mental disorder | 1 billion+; depression and anxiety cost $1 trillion a year | [WHO, Sep 2025] | | Treatment gap | About a third of people with depression get treatment, even in rich countries; India 70–92% untreated | [WHO], [PIB] | | Care capacity | 13 mental health workers per 100,000 people; 2% of health budgets | WHO | | Work | 12 billion working days lost a year to depression and anxiety | [WHO] | | Daily stress at work | 40% globally; US and Canada 50%, Europe 39%, South Asia 30%; 20% engaged | [Gallup 2026] | | Burnout symptoms | 22% across 30 countries; India highest at 59% (2023) | [McKinsey Health Institute] | | The infinite workday | An interruption every 2 minutes; meetings after 8 pm up 16% | [Microsoft Work Trend Index] | | Short on sleep | More than a third of US adults; sleep loss lowers positive mood and raises anxiety across 154 experiments | [CDC], [Palmer et al. 2024] | ### What people already do, and what works
-
AI is already a mental health tool. 16% of US adults used AI for mental health information in the past year (28% of 18–29s), and only 42% of them then saw a professional ([KFF]).
-
Apps lose people fast. Across 93 mental health apps, median retention was 3.9% at day 15 ([Baumel et al. 2019]).
-
Insomnia help works; stress apps barely do. Fully automated digital CBT for insomnia cut severity by SMD −0.71, holding at −0.76 after six months ([Hwang et al. 2025]). Stress apps managed g = 0.27, or 0.10 after correcting for publication bias ([Linardon et al. 2024]).
-
Wearable stress detection is not proven. Most datasets are small and under 24 hours ([Vos et al. 2023]).
### The safety line
-
US states ban AI therapy: Illinois up to $10,000 per violation ([IDFPR]) and Nevada up to $15,000 ([WSGR]). California has required suicide protocols since 1 January 2026 ([TechCrunch]).
-
Regulators and courts are active: the FTC is studying seven companion-chatbot makers ([FTC]). Character.AI settled five lawsuits and filed for bankruptcy in October 2026 ([CNN via KION]).
-
The EU bans emotion inference at work except for medical or safety reasons, so an employer stress monitor is out ([AI Act Art. 5]).
For Waldo this means: lead with sleep and workload, where evidence is strongest and calendar data gives something to act on. Show stress only as patterns the owner can correct. Keep crisis routing, no minors, and no engagement-chasing as defaults, not features.
## Health or productivity
People use AI more for their lives than for work, and that share is growing. No survey found asks people to choose health over productivity, though. So Waldo should be a life agent with health as its backbone, not a health-only app.
-
Personal use now dominates. Non-work ChatGPT messages grew from 53% to 73% between June 2024 and June 2025. "Health, fitness, beauty or self care" was 5.7% of all messages ([OpenAI usage paper]). On Claude, personal use rose from 35% to 42% of conversations ([Anthropic, March 2026]). On weekends it is just under 50%, with more emotional support and medical questions ([Anthropic, June 2026]).
-
Balance has passed pay. In Randstad's survey, work-life balance (83%) ranked above pay (82%) for the first time.
-
The top AI uses are personal. HBR's 2025 ranking, built mostly from Reddit posts, put therapy and companionship first, organising my life second and healthier living tenth, up from 75th ([HBR]).

## Does acting on health data help
The evidence supports a narrow claim. Feedback changes behaviour when it is actionable, yet few products make it actionable. Wearables are good at detecting sleep and weak at most other things. So Waldo has to prove that acting on body data beats simply asking "how did you sleep?"
-
Actionable feedback is what works, and it is rare. A review of 31 just-in-time diet and activity studies found only 5 gave actionable feedback. Of the 9 that tested efficacy, 4 improved behaviour, and 3 of those 4 used feedback that was continuously available, goal-oriented or actionable ([Schembre et al. 2018]).
-
Sensors detect sleep well and wake poorly. Seven consumer sleep trackers were tested against lab polysomnography in 34 adults. Sensitivity for sleep was 0.93 or higher, specificity for wake only 0.18–0.54, and sleep stages were inconsistent. All did worse on disrupted nights ([Chinoy et al. 2021]).
-
Waldo's July research reached the same place. It found the broad claim that people need an agent that "understands their body" is not established. The pain people actually show is the gap between health signals, commitments, constraints and "one trusted next action" (waldo-brain/01-Waldo/research/waldo-61-question-secondary-research-report-2026-07-14.md).
The opening is the action, not the insight. Coaches explain; an agent can move the meeting, order the lighter lunch or book the checkup, which is the actionable step the studies found missing.
## Who is in the space
Coaching is crowded: every wearable maker and four AI labs now explain health data. Agents that act mostly do not read the body. Muse is the only one with calendar, commerce and health connectors together. It is US-only, and its health actions are refill reminders and drafted messages to doctors ([HIT Consultant]). Google's coach is the one to watch. It is live in India at ₹499 a month but only coaches inside its app, and Gemini can already act elsewhere ([Google]). ChatGPT Health is US-only and does not order or book ([Analytics Insight]).
## Can Muse, dots or Instinct absorb it
They can copy data access and health chat in weeks. They cannot quickly copy months of one person's baselines, corrections and acted-on plans. Waldo's moat is that per-owner history plus the action loop, not access to the data. The plugin route is real but thin: the companies that became plugins inside big agents own scarce data, such as medical records or lab results.

-
Platforms retreat as often as they absorb. Google stopped new Fit sign-ups in 2024 and turns off the Fitbit Web API on 30 October 2026 ([Google]). Apple scrapped its AI health coach for falling short of a paid subscription ([BGR]).
-
The survivors own a loop. WHOOP is at a $1.1B run rate on bundled memberships ([Fitt]). Oura keeps about 85% of paid members after a year.
-
Clinical claims sink small players. Woebot retired its app in June 2025, citing the cost of FDA authorisation ([HLTH]).
### Waldo as a plugin for other agents
Possible, but as distribution once Waldo has owners, not as the business. OpenAI's app rules bar collecting protected health information and require verified organisations ([OpenAI]). Claude's directory requires the server to call your own APIs ([Anthropic]). Muse reviews each connector for security and legal fit ([Meta]). A Waldo plugin would hand an owner's own day context ("low-recovery day, heavy afternoon") to their other agents, with the owner's consent. It earns its place only after Waldo holds baselines the host cannot rebuild.
## The market in numbers
About 21 million people worldwide wear a tracker, would share its data with an AI, and already pay for a health or AI subscription. Roughly half are in the US. Winning 1–3% of them is 200,000–600,000 paying owners: $24–72 million a year at $10 a month. Every stage after the first is an estimate.
Working: watch, band and ring shipments of about 205 million a year, times a three-year life, is about 614 million devices. About 70% are still worn, so roughly 400 million people. Of those, 60% use AI and 25% would share data, giving 60 million. About 35% of those already pay, giving 21 million.

People already pay for body data:

The weakest numbers are the share-with-an-AI rates. They come from US surveys and are applied to every region.
### India: a huge need, thin spending on health software
Indians spend on health transactions (pharmacy, consults, gyms, protein) far more than on health apps. India therefore suits health-driven chores and commerce better than a health subscription.

One 30-day pilot answers the question this research cannot: does acting on body data change what people do, beyond asking them how they slept? Waldo's own research names the falsifier: "Health context changes neither user decisions nor outcomes beyond a brief self-report."
### The pilot
-
Who: 20 owners. 10 on Apple Health or Oura in the US or Europe, and 10 on Android Health Connect in India. All have a wearable and a busy calendar.
-
What Waldo does: three jobs only. Replan a depleted day (move one block, protect a recovery window). Fit food to the day. Run the care loop for refills, checkups and fasting lab tests. Sleep and workload lead, because that is where the evidence is strongest.
-
Design: within each person, alternate wearable-informed days with self-report-only days ("How did you sleep?" in one tap). The same proposals go out on both kinds of day, and only the input differs.
### Pass and kill thresholds (proposed)
| Measure | Pass | Kill or change course | | Proposal acceptance, wearable days vs self-report days | At least 1.3× higher | No difference: wearables stay optional and self-report carries the product | | Accepted health-driven actions | 3 or more per owner per week | Under 2: health is a feature, not the wedge; lead with chores | | Still active at day 30 | 60% or more (mental health apps keep 3.3%) | Under 40% | | "Waldo made a call I would not have made" | At least once a week for most owners | Rarely: the insight is not actionable enough | | False alarms from proactive health | Under one per owner per week, in shadow mode first | Above that: keep proactive health in shadow mode | If the pilot passes, health becomes the launch story: "the agent that acts on how you actually are". If it fails on the wearable comparison but passes on actions, Waldo still wins as a life agent that asks one good question each morning.
